# DIARY OF A WONKY ACORN — Content & Age-Appropriateness Guidelines

### The rulebook for every creative decision

> **Who this game is for:** players **11 and under** — and comfortable for the *youngest*
> end of that range too. Every line of dialogue, every scene, every character design, and
> every prop must pass the rules below. When in doubt, make it **gentler**.
>
> Read this *with* [SCRIPT.md](SCRIPT.md). SCRIPT.md says what the story *is*; this document
> says what it's *allowed to be*. If the two ever disagree, **this document wins.**

---

## 1. The promise

*Diary of a Wonky Acorn* is a **warm, funny, spooky-but-safe** adventure about moving house,
making a friend, and being brave. A child should finish it feeling **braver and happier**,
never frightened or upset.

The golden rule:

> **Spooky-fun is welcome. Real-world-scary is not.**

A silly cartoon villain who *wants to bag the roundest acorn for his collection* is spooky-fun.
A villain with a real weapon who *wants to hurt a child* is real-world-scary. We always pick
the first one.

---

## 2. 🟢 Green — always welcome

These are the heart of the game. Use them freely.

- **Warm family moments** — pancakes, bedtime, "sweetheart," a kiss on the cap.
- **Friendship** — Hazel scooping up Pico's books; loyalty; "we're friends now, that's decided."
- **Bravery that comes *with* fear** — Pico is scared and does the thing anyway. This is the
  whole point of the game.
- **Comedic peril** — chases where the hero is quick and the villain is clumsy and *always a
  half-step too slow*. Banana-peel logic, not danger.
- **Silly, theatrical villains** — vain, greedy, weird, over-the-top. We laugh *at* them.
- **Slapstick** — pratfalls, crashing into crates, getting tangled in a sheet.
- **Mild spookiness that resolves fast** — a too-quiet shop, a creak, a shiver… then a laugh.
- **A bully who reforms** — Brunk starts mean and *becomes a friend*. Show the turn.
- **Sadness that gets comforted** — homesickness, missing home, a wobbly first day — **always**
  with a parent, a friend, or a hug close behind.
- **Feelings shown through objects** — a jumper that "smells like home," *Treasure Island* on
  the windowsill, the diary.

---

## 3. 🟡 Amber — allowed, but only with the right technique

These can stay in the game **only** if handled exactly as described.

| Element | The rule that makes it OK |
|---|---|
| **Sadness / homesickness** | Must be **comforted on-screen** within the same scene (a parent, Hazel, Granny). Never leave a child player sitting in sadness. |
| **Tension / a chase** | Must resolve **quickly** *and* **with a laugh or a rescue**. No long, dread-building silences. Keep it bouncy. |
| **Spookiness** | **Cartoonish** only — a funny-weird shopkeeper, a cluttered shop. Atmosphere, never genuine dread. The scare should make you *giggle*, not flinch. |
| **A villain / "baddie"** | Must pass **The Cartoon-Menace Test** (§5). The threat is always about *taking/keeping*, never *harming*. The villain is silly underneath. |
| **Peril to the hero** | The hero is **never actually hurt** and is **never truly trapped** for long. Rescue or escape is always close. |
| **A "hint of creepy" character** | A *touch* of weird/uncanny is fine (twitchy, too-friendly, big eyes) **as long as it tips back into silly** and lands gentle enough for the youngest players. Quirky, not nightmare. |

## 4. 🔴 Red — never, anywhere

If you find any of these, it must be changed. No exceptions.

- **Real weapons** — knives, cleavers, blades, guns. (Silly props only: a sack, a net, a jar.)
- **Violence or the threat of physical harm** — hitting, blood, injury, "this won't hurt a bit."
- **"Eat you" / cannibalism framing** — no one wants to *eat*, cook, roast, or carve anyone.
  A villain may want to *collect* or *keep* you. Never consume you.
- **Grooming-style language toward a child** — "let me get a proper look at you," appraising a
  child's body ("lovely and round," "plump"), "come closer," "stay very still." This is a hard no
  even when the speaker is a "baddie."
- **Kidnapping / trafficking / "supplier" framing** — no "I answer to someone," no "they needed
  the acorns," no sense of a ring that *moves* children. A lone, silly villain is fine; a
  *system* that takes children is not.
- **Realistic captivity** — prison cells, iron bars, shackles, manacles, a bucket-for-a-toilet.
  (A villain getting comically shut in his own messy pantry is fine.)
- **Genuine dread or jump-scares** — no "(quiet… breathing… then BANG)" beats, no lights-out
  stalking, no escape-from-jail "HERE I COME" stingers.
- **Profanity / crude language / insults that wound** — keep it clean. Light teasing that gets
  resolved is fine; cruelty is not.
- **Romance or anything sexual**, including innuendo.
- **Alcohol, drugs, smoking, vaping.**
- **Mature real-world trauma** played for weight — abuse, self-harm, death of a loved one.

---

## 5. The Cartoon-Menace Test

Any villain, baddie, or scary beat must pass **all four**:

1. **Is the threat impossible / silly?** (You can't *really* get bagged and added to a squirrel's
   nut collection — that's nonsense, and nonsense is safe.)
2. **Is it about *taking / keeping*, never *harming*?** (Wants to *collect* you, not hurt you.)
3. **Does it resolve with a laugh, a rescue, or a pratfall?** (No beat ends on fear.)
4. **Would it sit comfortably beside *The Gruffalo*, *Fantastic Mr Fox*, or a Roald Dahl story?**
   (Cartoon menace for little kids — yes. A horror film — no.)

If a beat fails any one of these, soften it until it passes.

---

## 6. Tone north star — keep what's already great

The re-write is about **removing the sharp edges, not the soul.** Protect these:

- **Earnest first, funny underneath.** Pico means everything he says; the comedy is his smallness
  in a big world. (e.g. crying into his breakfast — *"Could do with a little less salt."*)
- **Humour amid sadness.** Every sad beat earns a gentle laugh nearby.
- **Courage isn't fearlessness.** The bravest thing in the game is done by the most timid character.
- **Show feeling through objects.** *Treasure Island*, the four moving boxes, the diary.
- **Childlike, not childish.** Pico is innocent but notices things — that's why he's a good detective.

---

## 7. Pre-flight checklist

Before any new scene, line, character, or prop ships, run it past this:

- [ ] No weapons, blades, or anything that could really hurt someone.
- [ ] No "eat / cook / carve" language; the villain *collects*, never consumes.
- [ ] No body-appraising or "come closer / stay still / let me look at you" lines.
- [ ] No kidnapping-ring / "I answer to someone" framing — the baddie acts alone and silly.
- [ ] No realistic prison, bars, shackles, or buckets.
- [ ] Any scary beat **resolves quickly with a laugh or a rescue.**
- [ ] Any sad beat is **comforted on-screen.**
- [ ] Clean language throughout; teasing always gets resolved.
- [ ] Passes the **Cartoon-Menace Test** (§5).
- [ ] Would I be happy with a 6-year-old playing this beat alone? If not, make it gentler.
